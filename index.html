How accurate is this?
  • Outdoors with open sky, iPhone GPS is usually within about 5–10 m (15–30 ft).
  • Indoors it often drifts 20–50 m+ (65–165 ft). Treat interior pins as notes on a sketch, not measured rooms.
  • The live map shows a blue circle for your phone's reported accuracy. The trail skips very rough readings and impossible jumps, and smooths out small jitter.
  • Pins average your last few seconds of readings. Stand still for 3–5 seconds before pinning for the best result.
  • iPhone can't record location while the screen is locked or Safari is in the background. Keep this screen open while you walk. That time isn't counted as recording time, and the trail shows a dashed gap.
  • No internet needed to record: GPS works offline and the map is drawn on the phone. After your first visit the app opens offline too.
  • If the GPS signal drops, the walk keeps recording and reconnects on its own. Your walk is saved every few seconds.

About & privacy

The short version

Perimeter Notes is a free, simple web page. There are no accounts and no server of ours. Your walks are saved only in Safari on your own iPhone. Nobody else can see them, including the person who made this app.

No analytics, no ads, no cookies, no tracking. No AI and no paid services.

What stays on your phone

Walks, GPS trails, pins and notes, labels, keywords, addresses and settings are all kept in this site's storage in Safari on this iPhone. They only leave it if you send them with Send diagram, Mail draft or Export backup.

What leaves your phone

  • The page itself. The app is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any website host, GitHub keeps standard visitor logs (such as IP address and browser type) when the page loads.
  • Address lookup. The first GPS point of each walk is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to find the street, city and state. If that fails, the same point goes to BigDataCloud's free lookup instead. Nothing else about the walk is sent. Each place is looked up once and remembered on the phone.
  • Street map. When you zoom in on the all-walks map with Streets on, map images are downloaded from OpenStreetMap's tile servers. That reveals which area you're looking at. Turn Streets off to stop it. Country and state outlines are built in and need no connection.
  • Voice dictation. The mic button uses Apple's speech recognition in Safari, which may send the audio to Apple under Apple's privacy policy. Typing never leaves the phone.
  • Only when you tap them: Open in Maps sends that pin's coordinates to Apple Maps. Send diagram and Mail draft share what you choose, with whoever you choose.

Those services see your IP address like any website does. Recording a walk itself needs no internet at all.

Keep your walks safe

  • Safari can erase website data if you clear history/website data, or if you haven't opened this site in Safari for about a week. Adding it to your Home Screen avoids the one-week rule.
  • Use Export backup now and then, and keep the file somewhere safe (Files, iCloud Drive, email to yourself).
  • Deleting the app from your Home Screen or changing phones doesn't move your walks. Export first, then Import on the new phone.

Tips

  • Add to Home Screen: in Safari tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It opens full-screen and works offline.
  • Keep the screen on while walking. iPhone stops GPS for web pages when the screen locks or Safari goes to the background.
  • Use the https:// address. iPhone only allows location and the microphone on secure pages.
  • Stand still for a few seconds before dropping a pin for the most accurate spot.

Delete everything

Erases all walks, notes, keywords, addresses and settings from this iPhone, plus the app's offline copy. This can't be undone, so export a backup first if you might want them.
